1) Meaning and purpose of the Address attribute

- What it is: Address is the street-level location of a hospital facility. It typically contains a street number, street name, street type or highway designation, and may include directional cues (north, south, east, west, or abbreviations like N, S, NW). It is used for geocoding, mapping, mail routing, and validation of location-related constraints.
- Typical components you may expect:
  - House number (e.g., 1300, 702, 8000)
  - Street name and possible suite/building info (e.g., south montgomery avenue, pine street, main st)
  - Street type or highway designation (e.g., avenue, street, st, blvd, highway)
  - Optional directional modifiers (northwest, north, south, nw, n)
  - In many datasets, the Address is a single free-form string without explicit separation into components
- Data quality implications:
  - Variability in abbreviations and suffixes (St vs Street, Ave vs Avenue, Blvd vs Boulevard)
  - Inclusion of highways or rural route names (e.g., “alabama highway 69”)
  - Case and punctuation inconsistencies
  - Possible typos or non-standard forms that hinder parsing or geocoding
  - Missing or inconsistent address parts can degrade downstream validation (ZipCode, City/State, County)

    - Optional directional words can appear as a separate token or prefix/suffix (N, S, E, W, NW, etc.)
